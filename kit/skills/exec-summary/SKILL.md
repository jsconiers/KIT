# Executive summary

One page for a reader who has two minutes and has to decide something. They will read the first
line, skim the rest, and act on what they understood. Write for that reader.

## What goes on the page

In this order, one short paragraph or a few bullets each. Leave a heading out only if the brief
says so; never leave it out because the answer is awkward.

1. **The answer.** One or two sentences: what you recommend and what you need. A reader who stops
   here should know what to do.
2. **The problem.** What is wrong or what changes, and why now. Say what it costs to do nothing,
   in money, risk, or time, if the sources say.
3. **The ask.** Exactly what leadership is asked to approve, fund, or decide, and by when. One
   ask per page. If there are two, say which comes first.
4. **Cost.** One-off and running cost, with the range, the currency, and the period. Say whether
   it's a rough order of magnitude, an estimate, or a quote. If no cost is stated, write "not
   stated" and say who can supply it.
5. **Risk.** The top three risks at most, each with its rating and the mitigation. Include the
   risk of not doing it.
6. **Decision needed.** The options leadership can choose between (usually two or three, one of
   them doing nothing or deferring), and which one you recommend, with the main reason.
7. **Next step.** What happens the day after the decision, who does it (by role), and the next
   date or gate.

## How to write it

- **Lead with the answer.** The recommendation goes first, not after the background. Each section
  also starts with its point, then the support.
- **Every number has a source.** Cite the document and section, for example [§5 Cost, DOC-0042
  v1.2], or the person's role and date. A number you can't source doesn't go on the page; write
  "not stated" instead. Never round a range into a single figure, and never invent one.
- **Keep the units.** Currency, period (one-off, per year, per month), and whether figures include
  tax or contingency, as the source states them.
- **No unexplained jargon or acronyms.** Spell out each acronym the first time, or replace it with
  plain words. If a technical term is the point, explain it in a short clause.
- **Plain words, short sentences.** Active voice. One idea per sentence. Cut "in order to",
  "leverage", "utilize", "going forward", and similar filler.
- **People by role,** not by name, unless the brief says otherwise.
- **One page.** If it doesn't fit, cut background first, then detail inside each section. Never cut
  the ask, the cost, the risk, or the decision.
- **Match the source's classification,** never lower. Put it at the top.
- **Gaps stay visible.** "Not stated" is better than a guess. List what's missing at the bottom so
  someone can fill it.

## Checklist

Before you hand it over, check every line:

- [ ] The first one or two sentences give the recommendation and the ask.
- [ ] The problem says why now and the cost of doing nothing (or "not stated").
- [ ] There is one clear ask, with a date for the decision.
- [ ] Cost shows a range, currency, period, and type (rough order of magnitude, estimate, or quote).
- [ ] No more than three risks, each with a rating and a mitigation.
- [ ] The options are listed, with the recommended one and the reason.
- [ ] The next step has an owner role and a date or gate.
- [ ] Every number and claim cites its source; nothing is invented.
- [ ] Every acronym is spelled out on first use, and no jargon is left unexplained.
- [ ] People appear by role. The classification is at the top and matches the source.
- [ ] It fits on one page.
- [ ] Gaps are written "not stated" and listed at the bottom.
